About temperature scales

°C

Celsius

Used in most countries for weather and everyday temperatures. Water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C.

°F

Fahrenheit

Commonly used in the United States. Water freezes at 32 °F and boils at 212 °F.

K

Kelvin

The scientific SI temperature scale. It begins at absolute zero, so Kelvin values never go below 0 K.

Temperature essentials

How conversion works

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32 °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9 K = °C + 273.15

Kelvin uses the same size degree as Celsius, but starts from absolute zero. Kelvin is written without a degree symbol.

Useful reference points

Reference point Celsius Fahrenheit Kelvin
Absolute zero −273.15 °C −459.67 °F 0 K
Water freezes 0 °C 32 °F 273.15 K
Room temperature 20 °C 68 °F 293.15 K
Water boils* 100 °C 212 °F 373.15 K

*At standard sea-level atmospheric pressure.

Did you know?

Celsius is named after Swedish astronomer Anders Celsius. Kelvin is important in science because it is an absolute temperature scale.
